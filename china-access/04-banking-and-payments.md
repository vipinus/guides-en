# 04 · Online banking, mobile banking and payments

## What a bank's risk control looks at

A bank does not look only at IP. It puts several signals together and scores them:

1. **Where the IP is registered**: an overseas IP raises the risk level straight away.
2. **SIM card**: a mobile banking app reads the SIM information, and a card from an overseas carrier counts against you.
3. **Device fingerprint**: a new device, or a jailbroken or rooted one.
4. **Behaviour**: a large transfer on the very first login from a new location.

So the common situation is: after switching to a China IP you can log in, but the transfer is still blocked. IP only solves the first item.

## What you can do

- After switching to a China IP, **do small transactions first** so that risk control gets used to this environment, then do large ones.
- Keep a Chinese SIM card and turn on roaming; both verification codes and the SIM check depend on it.
- Install the Chinese edition of the mobile banking app where possible; for store region problems, see [What to do when iOS won't let you install an app](../client/06-ios-app-store.md) in the sister repository.
- If you are blocked, do not keep retrying. Repeated failures trigger a temporary freeze, and calling the customer service line to unlock it is the fastest way.

## Alipay and WeChat Pay

Both are more tolerant of IP than banks are, and they usually work from an overseas IP, but accounts linked to an overseas card have limits on amounts and on where they can be used. When you need to pay with a Chinese card or transfer money to a Chinese merchant, switching to a China IP makes things much smoother.

## What not to do

Do not do banking on public Wi-Fi, whether or not you are going through the line. The line only changes your exit IP and does not encrypt anything for you beyond the bank; the banking app's own encryption is where the security comes from.
