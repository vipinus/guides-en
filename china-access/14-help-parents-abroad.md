# 14 · Setting things up for elderly relatives abroad: install once, then leave it alone

## The situation

Parents move abroad after retiring to live with their children, or spend long periods abroad visiting family. Their needs are very focused:

- Watching Chinese TV series, variety shows and news
- Listening to traditional opera, storytelling and audiobooks
- Reading Official Accounts and watching Channels in WeChat
- Occasionally opening a Chinese government service or medical insurance page

The difficulty is not technical. It is that **they cannot, and should not have to, learn the steps**. Any option that needs "one tap before each use" ends up with the children giving remote guidance once a week.

## The conclusion first

For elderly relatives, **a router is the only worry-free answer**.

The reason is straightforward: with the phone-app approach, the connection may need to be tapped again after every restart, every change of WiFi and every system update. A router stays connected from the moment it is plugged in, and the TV, tablet, smartphone and senior phone go through the line automatically once they are on the home WiFi, with no step in between that the elderly person has to perform.

## What to do in practice

1. Set up the router at your end (once only, ten-odd minutes)
2. Post it or carry it over, plug in the power and connect the network cable
3. Have the elderly relatives' devices connect to this router's WiFi

After that you do not need to do anything at all. All they see is "the TV can show Chinese programmes now"; they do not know, and do not need to know, what happens in between.

## A few details that save a lot of trouble

**Give the WiFi a name they recognise.** Do not use the default string of letters. Set it to something like "Living room", so that they can find it themselves when they change devices.

**Connect the TV by cable where you can.** In an elderly person's home the TV is often some distance from the router, and when the WiFi signal is weak the stuttering gets blamed on "this thing does not work well". One network cable saves a lot of explaining.

**One sentence is enough to tell them:** "If the internet is bad, unplug this little box and plug it back in." This sentence solves the great majority of occasional problems and is more practical than teaching them to read the indicator lights.

## Approaches we do not recommend

- **Do not** install a client that has to be switched on and off by hand on an elderly relative's phone
- **Do not** use an option whose configuration needs updating regularly
- **Do not** write the account and password on paper and stick it on the router (we really have seen this)

## If something does go wrong

You can also help them check remotely from abroad. Leave our contact details when you set things up, and when something goes wrong have them send one line, "I can't watch any more"; the rest is for you or support to work out, and they do not need to describe any technical details.
