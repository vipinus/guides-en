# 08 · Searching the literature, downloading papers, submitting manuscripts

## The situation

Doing research and writing papers in China, you cannot avoid these kinds of sites:

- Google Scholar, PubMed, arXiv, ResearchGate
- The official sites of Elsevier, Springer, Wiley and IEEE
- Journal submission systems (ScholarOne, Editorial Manager and others)
- Preprint platforms and conference submission systems

## First, tell two things apart: whether you can reach it, and whether you have access rights

These two are often mixed up, which leads to wasted effort.

**Whether you can reach it**: whether the website opens at the network level. Not opening, spinning and timing out belong here, and changing the exit IP solves it.

**Whether you have access rights**: the literature is paid, and depends on your institution's subscription. The sign of this one is that **the page opens normally**, but when you click download you are told to pay or to log in with an institutional account. At that point no number of region changes will help — what you need is your university's access rights.

How to tell: if the page opens and you can see the abstract but cannot download, it is an access-rights problem, not a line problem.

## Institutional access and the line can be stacked

Universities generally give you access in two ways: a campus VPN, or an off-campus access system. Neither conflicts with the cross-border line, and they can be used at the same time:

1. First use the line so that you can connect to the university's servers
2. Then use the university's VPN / off-campus system to get database access

The reverse order usually does not work, because the university's system itself may be in China or may be abroad.

## Submission systems need extra stability

Submitting a manuscript is like registering for something: a situation where **a break midway means starting from the beginning**. Uploading the manuscript, replacing the blind-review files, confirming the proofs — every step can be tens of megabytes. We suggest:

- Use a computer, not a phone
- Do not switch region at any point
- Before uploading large files, confirm that the connection is stable, and do not do it where the signal is poor

## A detail about Google Scholar

Google Scholar is fairly sensitive to **request frequency**. If you scrape in bulk with a script, or page through many results in a short time, you will be asked to complete a verification. This is not a line problem; slowing down is enough.

## Choosing a region

Academic sites are mostly in Europe and North America, but **latency has little effect on reading the literature** — you are mainly opening pages and downloading PDFs, not playing games. Nearby regions such as Japan and Singapore usually feel better, because the round trip is quick and pages load briskly.

The cases where you really need to choose Europe or North America are: some publishers apply regional restrictions by the source of access, or your institutional account is tied to a particular region. Switch when you run into it.
