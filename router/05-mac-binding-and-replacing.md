# 05 · MAC binding and replacing a router

## Why there is binding

Router access is bound to the account by hardware address (MAC): the first time you log in with a given router, the server records that router under your account, and from then on only that router can use the account's router feature. This is the usual way of preventing an account from being resold to several households.

## Rules

- One account is bound to only one router.
- There is no restriction the other way round: the same router can be used by several accounts one after another (family members logging in with different accounts).
- Clients such as phones and computers are not subject to this restriction; they are counted by the number of devices online at the same time.

## Replacing a router

1. Log in to the website, find your bound router in the "Router" section, and click "Unbind".
2. Log in to the same account on the new router; it binds automatically.

If the old router is broken or not at hand, just unbind it; it does not need to be online.

## A common misunderstanding

A "binding conflict" is usually two routers in the house both logged in to the same account. Keep one, log out on the other, then unbind and bind again once.
