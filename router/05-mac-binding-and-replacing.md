# 05 · MAC binding and replacing a router

## Why there is binding

Router access is bound to the account by hardware address (MAC): the first time you log in with a given router, the server records that router under your account, and from then on only bound routers can use the account's router feature. That way the account's router feature stays yours alone.

## Rules

- Routers one account can bind, by plan: Basic 1, Standard 2, Pro 3, Elite 4, Enterprise 5, Ultimate 6. While there is a free slot, a new router is bound automatically the first time it connects.
- There is no restriction the other way round: the same router can be used by several accounts one after another (family members logging in with different accounts).
- Clients such as phones and computers are not subject to this restriction; they are counted by the number of devices online at the same time.

## Replacing a router

1. Free slot left on your plan: log in to the same account on the new router; it connects and binds automatically, no unbinding needed.
2. All slots used: log in to the website and open the "Router" section. Under the "Build Router Firmware" heading is the binding list (marked "x / y routers"), one row per router with its model and the last digits of its MAC. Click "Unbind" at the end of the old router's row, and the new router then connects by itself.

If the old router is broken or not at hand, just unbind it; it does not need to be online.

## A common misunderstanding

A "binding conflict" usually means all router slots are used: unbind one you no longer use in the "Router" section, or upgrade your plan.
